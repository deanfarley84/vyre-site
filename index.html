Common questions

Straight answers first.

What is the difference between VYRE OS and VYRE Embedded?

VYRE OS is for merchants: it reviews your own payment estate, quantifies what you are losing and shows how to recover it. VYRE Embedded is for payment providers: the same intelligence layer, licensed so a gateway, PSP or acquirer can advise its own merchants within its own remit.

I already have a dashboard. Why do I need Vyre?

You have level 1: a dashboard that ingests your data and shows it back to you. If your provider also retries or re-routes failed transactions, that is level 2. Vyre is levels 3 and 4. Level 3 tells you what is actually wrong, what it is costing the business in pounds, and exactly how to fix it. Level 4 verifies the recovery once the fix is live. Data on its own is not a decision. There is also an incentive question: a provider's routing and retry logic is built around its own rails and margins, not your P&L. VYRE OS is fully agnostic and independent. It takes no money, referral fee or revenue share from any provider, so the recommendation only ever tracks your numbers.

What is payment leakage?

Payment leakage is revenue a merchant should have received on transactions that were attempted, but did not receive because of avoidable declines, hidden FX spread, poor routing, missing retries, unchallenged chargebacks or absent local payment methods. Industry benchmarks put it at 1 to 3% of revenue for cross-border merchants.

Is Vyre independent?

Yes. Vyre is independently owned and provider-agnostic: not tied to any PSP, acquirer or orchestrator, and built to work with whatever stack you already run. On merchant reviews, Vyre takes no fees, kickbacks or referral payments from PSPs, acquirers or orchestrators; the merchant pays, against recovery. VYRE Embedded is a separate line, licensed to payment providers for use with their own merchants, and the two are never mixed on the same engagement.

Does Vyre change anything in my payment flow?

No. Vyre does not reroute transactions or change your stack. It validates the data, tells you what is not working, where volume should go instead, why, how to make the change and what the recovery is worth, then verifies the result once you make it.
